# SERIES_BIBLE.md — Editorial Identity

## The One Sentence That Governs Everything

**This series makes complete beginners feel awakened — not overwhelmed — by AI.**

Every word, sentence, section, diagram, and quiz question must serve this single purpose.

---

## Voice

The voice of this series is Kelvin M speaking directly to a curious, intelligent person who has no technical background. It is not a textbook. It is not a blog post. It is not a lecture. It is a conversation between a knowledgeable friend and someone who genuinely wants to understand.

**The voice is:**
- Warm but authoritative
- Plain but not dumbed-down
- Confident but never arrogant
- Encouraging but never patronising
- Academic but never cold

**The reader should always feel:**
- Respected — they are not being talked down to
- Included — this content is for them, not despite them
- Capable — they can understand this
- Curious — they want to read the next edition

## Source Language Patterns — Writing Style Derived from Tweets

The PDF series should mirror the language energy and patterns found in the source Twitter/X posts — adapted for a beginner audience and stripped of slang. These patterns make the content feel native to readers who consume social media educational content.

### Language Patterns to Adopt

| Pattern | Example from Sources | How to Use in PDFs |
|---------|---------------------|-------------------|
| **Direct second-person address** | "You do not need to keep up with every AI update" | Always write to "you" — never "one" or "the reader" |
| **Personal experience as proof** | "If I were starting AI today, this is exactly what I'd do" | Use real stories (attributed or anonymized) to anchor concepts |
| **Numbers and specificity** | "30-day roadmap", "4 AI side hustles", "10 things humans do better" | Use specific numbers in headings and lists — they create authority |
| **Framework/roadmap thinking** | "The Operator Toolkit", "Beginner Roadmap to Master AI Agents" | Structure concepts as named frameworks or step-by-step roadmaps |
| **How-to and what-to framing** | "How to stop feeling behind in AI", "How to build a prompt for anything" | Lead sections with "how" and "what" — action-oriented, not passive |
| **Conversational rhythm** | Short punchy sentence. Then a longer one that unpacks the idea and gives it room to breathe. Then short again. | Vary sentence length deliberately — short for impact, long for explanation |
| **Urgency without fear-mongering** | "The window is closing", "the people who started early built entire careers" | Create motivation through opportunity, not anxiety |
| **Analogy-first explanations** | "A chatbot is like a calculator. An AI agent is like an employee." | Always lead with the analogy, then follow with the technical explanation |
| **Practical, outcome-focused language** | "Use this to build", "the skill that changed how I use Claude" | Frame everything in terms of what the reader will be able to DO |
| **Confident declarations** | "This is one of the most important transitions happening in AI right now" | State importance directly — do not hedge with "might be" or "arguably" |

### Language Patterns to Avoid (from Sources)

These appear in the raw tweets but should NOT be carried into the PDFs:

| Avoid | Why | Found In |
|-------|-----|----------|
| "Bro", "bruh", "ngl" | Slang — does not match academic-accessible tone | Various posts |
| Excessive caps for emphasis | "THIS is what nobody talks about" — use formatting instead | Thread openers |
| Hashtag language | "#AI #MachineLearning" — feels like marketing | All raw posts |
| Aggressive sales framing | "Stop wasting hours", "you suck at" | Posts 2, 4 |
| Unverified hype | "$10K/month guaranteed" — revenue claims without proof | Posts 9, 21, 29 |
| Tool-specific worship | "Claude is the GOAT" — remain tool-neutral | Various posts |
| Internet slang abbreviations | "imo", "tbh", "afaik" | Various posts |

### Sentence Cadence Guide

The tweets teach us a rhythm. Mirror this cadence in all body sections:

1. **Hook line** — Short, surprising, direct. (1 sentence, under 15 words)
2. **Expansion** — Unpack the hook with context and detail. (2-3 sentences, natural flow)
3. **Proof point** — Analogy, example, or data. (1-2 sentences)
4. **Takeaway** — What this means for the reader. (1 sentence, confident and clear)

**Example cadence:**
> AI does not read your words the way you do. When you type a sentence, AI looks at every word and weighs how much each word helps it understand the others. Think of it like highlighting the connections in a spider web — each thread matters, but some matter more than others. This process is what makes AI understand meaning, not just letters.

## The Aha Moment Rule

**Every edition must open with a real-life analogy that creates an aha moment before any technical explanation begins.**

The analogy must:
- Require zero technical knowledge to understand
- Come from everyday life that any global reader can relate to
- Make the reader feel they already partially understand the concept
- Be resolved by the end of the first page

**Structure template:**
> "You already know how to [familiar everyday action]. When you do this, you are [process]. AI does something remarkably similar — it [parallel process]. The difference is [key distinction]. That is the foundation of everything you will learn in this edition."

**Test:** If a 13-year-old who has never used a computer cannot understand the analogy, rewrite it.

## Mandatory Sections — Every Edition Must Contain All of These

1. **Cover page** — series name, edition number + title, "by Kelvin M — AI Educator & Researcher"
2. **Hook / Aha moment opener** — real-life analogy, 2–3 paragraphs
3. **Learning objectives** — 3–5 bullet points, "By the end of this edition, you will be able to..."
4. **Table of contents** — auto-generated from section headings
5. **Body sections** — minimum 3 major sections, each containing explanatory text + example + callout + step-by-step or table
6. **Key takeaways** — 3–5 standalone sentences
7. **Glossary** — every technical term defined in plain language
8. **Mini quiz** — exactly 10 questions (3 MCQ + 2 T/F + 2 Fill in blank + 3 Short answer)
9. **Reflection question** — alternating Personal / Conceptual / Practical
10. **CTA block** — all six CTAs in fixed order
11. **Next edition teaser** — 2–3 sentences ending on a cliffhanger

## Callout Box Rules

Four types of callout boxes are permitted. No other types.

| Type | Label | When to Use | Maximum Per Edition |
|------|-------|-------------|---------------------|
| Did You Know? | DID YOU KNOW? | Surprising fact or counterintuitive truth | 3 |
| Key Fact | KEY FACT | Important definition or concept anchor | 3 |
| Real World | IN THE REAL WORLD | Concrete application example | 2 |
| Watch Out | WATCH OUT | Common misconception to correct | 2 |

**Callout box rules:**
- Maximum 3 lines of text per callout box
- No callout box may contain a list — lists go in the body
- No callout box may replace a body section — they supplement, never substitute
- Every callout must be able to stand alone — readable without the surrounding body text

---

## Step-by-Step Breakdown Rules

Every edition must contain at least one step-by-step section showing a practical application of the concept.

**Rules:**
- Minimum 3 steps, maximum 7 steps
- Each step begins with an action verb — "Open", "Type", "Click", "Notice", "Observe"
- Each step is one action only — no compound steps
- Steps must be doable by someone with only a smartphone
- Steps must be concrete — "Open ChatGPT and type: 'Explain machine learning in one sentence'" not "Experiment with AI tools"

---

## Comparison Table Rules

Every edition must contain at least one comparison table.

**Permitted table types:**
- Before AI vs After AI
- How humans do X vs How AI does X
- Concept A vs Concept B
- Common misconception vs Reality

**Table rules:**
- Maximum 3 columns
- Maximum 8 rows
- Column headers always present
- No merged cells
- No cell may contain more than 2 lines of text

## Forbidden Phrases

Never use any of the following phrases in any edition:

| Forbidden | Why | Replace With |
|-----------|-----|--------------|
| "Simply put" | Condescending | Just say it simply without flagging it |
| "As you know" | Assumes knowledge the reader may not have | Remove entirely |
| "Obviously" | Condescending | Remove entirely |
| "Just" (minimising) | e.g. "just click here" — minimises difficulty | Remove "just" |
| "Revolutionary" | Overused hype word | Use specific description instead |
| "Game-changer" | Cliché | Describe what specifically changes |
| "Cutting-edge" | Overused tech cliché | Use "current" or "recent" |
| "Leverage" | Corporate jargon | Use "use" |
| "Utilize" | Unnecessary formality | Use "use" |
| "In today's fast-paced world" | Meaningless filler opener | Start with the actual content |
| "Artificial intelligence is transforming..." | Overused opener | Never open an edition this way |
| "Don't worry" | Patronising | Build confidence through content, not reassurance |
| "This might seem complicated but..." | Pre-frames failure | Never pre-apologise for content |
| "Trust me" | Undermines authority | Demonstrate, don't assert |
| "In conclusion" | Weak section opener | Use the section heading instead |

## Complexity Sequence Rules

**The series must follow a strict complexity gradient across all 20+ editions.**

No edition may assume knowledge from an edition that has not been published yet. Every technical term used must either:
1. Have been defined in a previous edition (reference it: "As we covered in Edition 02..."), or
2. Be defined in the glossary of the current edition

**Complexity levels:**

| Editions | Level | Reader State |
|----------|-------|--------------|
| 01–05 | Foundational | Reader knows nothing about AI |
| 06–10 | Core Concepts | Reader understands what AI is |
| 11–15 | Applications | Reader understands how AI works |
| 16–20+ | Advanced & Critical | Reader can use AI and wants to think deeply about it |

## Viral Writing Rules

Every edition must be written with video conversion in mind. Each of the following must be present and clearly identifiable:

| Element | Where It Lives | Purpose |
|---------|---------------|---------|
| Hook sentence | First 2 sentences of the hook section | First 3 seconds of video — must stop the scroll |
| Quotable one-liner | At least 1 per body section | Text overlay in video — must work as a standalone statement |
| Storytelling arc | Full edition structure | Beginning (problem/analogy) → Middle (explanation) → Revelation (aha moment resolved) |
| Shocking/counterintuitive fact | At least 1 per edition, ideally in a callout box | Viral share trigger |
| Step-by-step list | Step-by-step section | Converts directly to video slides |
| Cliffhanger ending | Next edition teaser | Last line of video — drives follow |

**The hook sentence test:** Read the first two sentences out loud. Would you stop scrolling on TikTok if you heard this? If not, rewrite.

**The one-liner test:** Pull any key takeaway. Can it stand alone as a shareable quote card? If not, rewrite it.

## Quiz Writing Rules

**Exactly 10 questions per edition. Exact format:**
- Questions 1–3: Multiple Choice (A, B, C, D) — testing factual recall
- Questions 4–5: True / False — testing conceptual understanding
- Questions 6–7: Fill in the blank — testing key definitions
- Questions 8–10: Short answer (1–2 sentences) — testing application and reflection

**Quiz rules:**
- No trick questions
- All correct answers must be found within the body of the edition
- MCQ distractors must be plausible — not obviously wrong
- Fill-in-the-blank answers must be single words or short phrases (maximum 4 words)
- Short answer questions must have a clear correct direction — not purely subjective
- Answer key stored in `answer_key.md` — never published in the PDF
