### Research-Based Additions (Claude Knowledge)

- **1950 — Alan Turing asks "Can machines think?"** The Turing Test proposes that if a machine can fool a human into thinking it is human, it can be considered intelligent.
- **1956 — The Dartmouth Conference.** The term "artificial intelligence" is officially coined. Researchers are wildly optimistic — they predict human-level AI within a generation.
- **1960s-70s — Early enthusiasm.** First chatbot (ELIZA, 1966) makes people think machines can have conversations. Early rule-based systems can play chess and solve simple puzzles.
- **1974-1980 — The first "AI Winter."** Progress stalls. Computers are too slow, data is too scarce. Funding dries up. AI is dismissed as overhyped.
- **1980s — Expert systems.** AI bounces back with rule-based programs that mimic human experts in narrow fields (medical diagnosis, mineral prospecting). Businesses invest billions.
- **1987-1993 — The second AI Winter.** Expert systems prove brittle and expensive to maintain. Funding collapses again.
- **1997 — IBM's Deep Blue beats world chess champion Garry Kasparov.** A landmark moment that shows AI can outperform humans in specific tasks.
- **2012 — Deep learning breakthrough.** A neural network crushes the ImageNet competition, recognizing images far better than any previous system. This is the moment modern AI begins.
- **2016 — AlphaGo beats world Go champion.** Go is vastly more complex than chess. This proves AI can handle problems too complex for brute-force calculation.
- **2022 — ChatGPT launches.** Overnight, AI goes from a researcher's tool to something anyone can use. 100 million users in two months.
- **2023-2026 — The current era.** AI can write, code, create images, generate video, analyze data, and carry on conversations. The tools are getting cheaper, faster, and more accessible every month.
