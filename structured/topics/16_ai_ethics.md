### Research-Based Additions (Claude Knowledge)

- **Bias in AI:** AI reflects the biases in its training data. Real examples: hiring AI that discriminated against women (Amazon, 2018), facial recognition with higher error rates for darker skin (MIT study), loan algorithms that disadvantaged minority communities.
- **Privacy:** AI requires data. Whose data? Given with consent? Can you opt out? The EU's AI Act (2024) and similar regulations are attempting to set boundaries.
- **Accountability:** When AI makes a wrong decision — denies a loan, misdiagnoses a condition, causes an accident — who is responsible? The developer? The company? The user?
- **Deepfakes and misinformation:** AI can create realistic fake images, audio, and video. This has implications for trust, democracy, and personal reputation.
- **Environmental cost:** Training a single large AI model can produce as much carbon as five cars over their entire lifetimes. AI's environmental footprint is growing.
