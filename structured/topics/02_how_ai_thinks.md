### Research-Based Additions (Claude Knowledge)

- **Pattern recognition:** At its core, AI "thinks" by recognizing patterns. It has seen millions of examples and learned what typically follows what. When you ask it a question, it is not "understanding" the way a human does — it is predicting the most likely response based on patterns.
- **Training is like studying for an exam:** AI is shown millions of examples (the textbook), tests itself, checks its answers, and adjusts. Over time, it gets better at predicting the right answer. The key difference: AI can study millions of examples in hours, while a human would take years.
- **Neural networks (simplified):** AI is loosely inspired by the human brain. It uses layers of mathematical calculations (called neural networks) that pass information from one layer to the next, each layer refining the understanding. Like a game of telephone, but one that gets more accurate at each step instead of less.
- **The "black box" reality:** Even AI researchers cannot always explain exactly why an AI made a specific decision. This is called the "black box" problem and is one of the biggest challenges in AI today.

## Key Analogies for This Topic

1. **Bike riding feedback loop** (Post 3) — pedal, wobble, fall, adjust, repeat. AI does this millions of times per second.
2. **Puzzle pieces** (Post 11) — AI chops text into tokens before processing, like cutting a sentence into pieces
3. **Short-term memory** (Post 11) — context window is like remembering only the last 50 pages
4. **Highlighting connections** (Post 11) — attention mechanism highlights which words relate to each other
