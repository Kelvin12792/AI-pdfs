### Research-Based Additions (Claude Knowledge)

- **Where training data comes from:** Books, websites, Wikipedia, scientific papers, code repositories, news articles, social media posts, government documents. Modern LLMs are trained on datasets containing trillions of words.
- **Why training data matters more than the algorithm:** Two identical AI architectures trained on different data will produce completely different results. Data is the ingredient list. The algorithm is the recipe. Bad ingredients make bad food, no matter how good the chef.
- **Data bias:** If training data contains biases (gender stereotypes, racial prejudice, cultural assumptions), the AI will reproduce and sometimes amplify those biases. This is not a bug — it is a direct reflection of the data.
- **Labeled vs. unlabeled data:** Labeled data has human-assigned tags ("this photo contains a cat"). Unlabeled data is raw, untagged content. Labeling is expensive and slow — often done by thousands of human workers.
- **Data privacy concerns:** AI models may have trained on personal data, copyrighted work, or private conversations posted online. This raises serious ethical and legal questions that are still being debated globally.
- **Synthetic data:** AI can now generate its own training data — AI creating examples for other AI to learn from. This is both a solution to data scarcity and a potential source of compounding errors.
