### Edition 02 — How AI Thinks
**Complexity:** Foundational
**Reader assumption:** Knows AI exists and is not afraid of it. Does not know how it works at all.

**Available content from this batch:**
- Feedback loops from cybernetics: set a goal, take action, observe results, compare to goal, adjust — this is exactly how many AI systems learn (Post 3)
- "Learning to ride a bike" feedback loop analogy — AI tries, checks, adjusts, repeats, millions of times per second (Post 3)
- Growth stages: toddler sees world in simple terms (good/bad) then develops nuance — AI develops similarly from simple yes/no to shades of grey (Post 3)
- Attention mechanism: AI weighs how much every word helps it understand every other word in a sentence — "The cat sat on the mat" example (Post 11)
- Tokenization: AI chops text into puzzle pieces called tokens before processing (Post 11)
- Context windows: AI's working memory — like only remembering the last 50 pages of a book (Post 11)
- AI understands relationships and structure, not just individual data points — the detective examining a crime scene analogy (Post 25)
- Information overload: when given too much unstructured input, AI output quality drops — same as humans (Post 6)
- Progressive disclosure: AI loads relevant knowledge as needed, like textbooks in a backpack (Post 14)

**Source posts:** 3, 6, 11, 14, 20, 22, 25
**Content gaps:**
- No visual or diagram-ready explanation of a neural network at a basic level
- Missing a concrete walkthrough of "you type X, and here is exactly what happens inside the AI, step by step"
- No explanation of how AI is different from traditional computer programs (if-then rules vs pattern learning)

**Strongest analogy available:** "Learning to ride a bike is a feedback loop: you pedal, wobble, fall, adjust your balance, and try again. AI learns the same way — it tries, checks its results, adjusts, and tries again. The difference? AI can do this millions of times per second." (Post 3)
